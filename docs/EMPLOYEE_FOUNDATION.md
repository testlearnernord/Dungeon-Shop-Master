# Employee Management V0.001 A–E — Tettel

## Owner vision

Tettel is a prototype name. He is nearly identical to the current dungeon Ghoul, but peaceful: he repeatedly returned to Galvik even after being beaten, was hidden in the cellar because customers were frightened, and learned shelf stocking and cashier work by decades of observation. Reliable, slower than the player, never receives tips. The reference concept image is not an asset design contract.

## Implemented foundation

- One known employee by default, no hire price or wages in this stage; no combat membership. Reuse existing Ghoul model and idle/walk clips, no new rig.
- Saved modular definitions, supported tasks, priorities Off/1–9. A/B defaults all Off to avoid unrequested interference. Lower number wins; equal priority favors the older continuously available task type, then stable task/target ordering. Keep equivalent current target when valid.
- Real work opportunities: physically waiting head of checkout queue, configured shelf (including empty assigned slots) with spare capacity and owned stock, actual active credit request with service claim. No phantom tasks for unavailable stock.
- Physical fixed-step movement at 1.47 m/s (70% of nominal customer walking speed), same world clock and existing connected route search, private-room geometry and real ramp height. Open portrait to permit travel; blocked routes wait and retry. Idle returns to available cellar home. No teleport through closed doors. Employee routes use the shared grid search with current customer positions as temporary obstacles; blocked movement triggers bounded replanning at the existing one-second cadence.
- Employee UI J or Employees button, persisted priorities and checkpoint. A/B alone had no work effects. C2, D and E now implement stock reservations, physical service and manual takeover as described below.

### Owner-approved shelf assignment contract (C1 implementation)

Galvik can assign a specific sellable item to each individual shelf slot independently of its current quantity. This is a general shop-management feature, usable without an employee. The assignment persists until Galvik explicitly clears or changes it, including when the last unit is sold or removed, when stock is unavailable, across save/reload and when the same shelf is moved. Employees may restock assigned slots but must never choose, clear or change their assignments.

Proposed implementation safeguards: retain the canonical stock and cart authority; an assignment is not stock and cannot itself satisfy shopping demand. Reject incompatible assignments or clearing while physical or cart-reserved goods remain, rather than deleting or converting goods. An empty assigned slot is a valid restocking target only when the matching stock is available and the physical task can proceed. Preserve existing cart-return capacity checks. C1 uses the existing slot `itemId` as the persistent assignment and `quantity` as physical stock. Middle-click on a reachable slot assigns the selected quickbar product, toggles off the same assignment, or clears an empty assigned slot with an empty quickbar selection. Quickbar product references can be bound even at zero inventory. A small non-pickable paper slip on the physical shelf shows the assigned product at any stock count. Initial manual stocking of an unassigned slot also assigns it. Left/right-click retain stock/remove semantics. Assignment and manual transfer are available without employee work. C1 and C2 are merged after green PR CI; physical visual acceptance remains pending. The existing v46 slot shape remains valid, including an unassigned empty slot with previously reserved customer goods; the cart lock still protects it.

## C2 physical restocking

- The existing bag remains the single owned merchandise pool; the cellar chest is a physical employee collection/return dock, not a second inventory.
- Claim only real matching available units up to one shelf's remaining capacity. The saved reservation is a subset of bag stock. Manual transfer, discard and bag-funded credit cannot consume reserved units. Manual stocking of the target may cancel an uncollected claim.
- Physical collection, delivery and return use the existing employee route and clock. Collection/return take 20 ticks; placement takes 20 ticks per reserved unit. Cargo is visible only after collection. A stale assignment, full shelf, new unpaid customer cart or disabled priority cancels uncollected work or triggers a physical return. No remote work on missed ticks.
- All transfers use the canonical shelf mutation; no gold or customer equipment changes. Cashier or charity may preempt uncollected restocking if it has strictly higher priority. Carried goods finish delivery or return before changing tasks. Charity uses the existing credit authority.

## D physical cashier

- A real first customer must own a current unpaid cart and a checkout service claim at the same front queue point used by Galvik's register. Tettel must arrive at the actual staff dock and remain ready for 60 uninterrupted world ticks (3 unpaused seconds, initial tuning).
- Settlement calls the same `payReservedShopCart` transaction as manual checkout. The existing `manual` sale channel and receipt ID represent staffed checkout, whether Galvik or Tettel serves. The employee's name is recorded in the sale message; no tips are awarded by the employee path. Customer departure, visit identity, revenue, equipment and cart ownership use the existing authority.
- Galvik's open register session takes precedence. A changed customer/counter, disabled priority, lost physical claim or discontinuous work clock resets the employee timer. A completed transaction cannot be repeated.
- The timer is saved in the strict v49 employee checkpoint. C2 reservations are unchanged; no second inventory, register or customer queue.

## E credit demand investigation

The existing begging mechanic is a full-price debt, not a gift. Earlier scripted campaigns declined unaffordable needs immediately, so zero granted credits cannot establish zero demand. The bounded audit compares the same 30-day Forge setup at seeds 42 and 7351 with `forge` (decline) and `forge-credit` (approve eligible stocked requests via `issueShopCredit`). Record natural quotes, availability and requested SKU, granted units and sticker debt, resolved visits, gold, survival and progression. Forged merchandise conservation includes credited units. These idealized immediate decisions measure possible demand and economic exposure, not pedestrian waiting, Tettel's throughput or actual human choices. All four diagnostic runs passed: 23 requests (22 bread, one healing herb), all stocked, nine approved in the credit treatments. Owner approved keeping the system and enabling opt-in employee credit. Defaults remain Off. No new runtime economy or save format.

## E physical credit service

- A saved, unexpired `cannot-afford` petition must have a living at-shop hero, matching requested item and matching credit/overflow/roam claim. Work can target the reserved destination of a walking petitioner, but service requires the actual actor to be stationary there. Tettel targets an unoccupied reachable point adjacent to that claim, not the hero's own position.
- Revalidate the actual quote, inventory and physical position at arrival. Commit one item via `issueShopCredit`, attribute the receipt to Tettel and remove the resolved request through the existing scheduler. No new revenue, gift policy or inventory. Manual conversation excludes that hero; expiration, departure, changed claim and disabled priority cancel service.
- Strictly higher charity priority may interrupt uncollected restocking, but carried stock must finish or return. Existing saved route is sufficient; the initial handover is instantaneous at the adjacent dock, like manual credit. No separate cargo pickup animation or timer for credit; physical appearance and throughput remain F acceptance.
